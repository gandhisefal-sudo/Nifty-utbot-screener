Tiles marked ≈ use the average of their stocks because the index's own history wasn't available for that period.
The other two tabs are built from the Nifty 500, grouped by NSE's sector classification. A sector's move is the average move of its stocks (each stock counts equally), so it shows how the typical stock in the sector did. The green/red bar under each sector is its breadth: the share of its stocks that are up. Today uses live quotes (refreshed every minute while the market is open); 1 week and 1 month use NSE's end-of-day closing prices. Click a sector to see every stock in it. Trading with the strongest sector (buys) or the weakest one (shorts) tends to give signals more follow-through.
🔭 Radar: stocks to watch before and right after the open
Can you know in advance what FIIs, DIIs or mutual funds will buy? No. Their orders aren't public before they trade, and anyone claiming to know them is guessing or breaking the law. What you can see is the trail big money leaves: unusual volume, high delivery (shares actually taken home, not day-traded), tight ranges near a breakout, and disclosed bulk and block deals. The fastest warning for a move like ITI's comes in the first minutes after 9:15. Keep ⚡ Live volume shocks open with alerts on.

When to expect the first confirmed signal (NSE opens 9:15 IST): a signal is confirmed only when its candle closes. The earliest possible time is therefore 9:16 on 1m, 9:20 on 5m, 9:30 on 15m, 9:45 on 30m, 10:15 on 1h and 10:30 on 75m. Signals from candles that start before 9:30 are flagged ⚠ opening candle. Those candles carry the overnight gap and the opening rush, so volume and VWAP look extreme and many signals fail. A practical routine is to wait out the first 15 minutes, then take 5m signals from about 9:30–9:45 onward, using the 15m signal (first one at 9:30) as the trend filter. Avoid new entries after about 2:45 pm, and square off by 3:15 pm.
Why TradingView shows a signal the scanner doesn't: TradingView keeps every past BUY/SELL label on the chart, so a buy from two weeks ago is still visible. The scanner, by default, lists only fresh flips from the last 3 candles that also pass your confirmation level, because an old signal is usually no longer a good entry. Use "Check a stock" to see any stock's current UT Bot state and signal date, or raise "Signal age" to list older signals.
Matching TradingView: the UT Bot, Supertrend, VWAP, EMA, RSI and MACD calculations follow TradingView's formulas, and candles are anchored at 9:15 IST like TradingView's NSE charts. With the same settings and "Closed candles only" ticked, signals should line up with the "UT Bot Alerts" indicator on the same timeframe. Check your indicator's settings: TradingView shows them next to the name, so "UT Bot Alerts 2 14" means Key Value 2, ATR Period 14. Enter the same two numbers here. The tool's default is 2 and 14 (see the settings test below). TradingView's own UT Bot default is 1 and 10, so set your chart indicator to 2 / 14 too, or change the values here to match your chart. Yahoo adds NSE's official closing price as an extra flat candle at about 3:28 pm. The tool removes it, as TradingView does, so the last candle of the day closes at the last traded price. Small differences can still happen. Prices here come from Yahoo Finance, not NSE's own feed, so a candle's high/low can differ by a few paise. A price sitting right on the stop line can then flip one candle earlier or later. Always confirm on the chart before trading.
How signals work: A BUY means the UT Bot flipped bullish within the last 3 candles of today's session, and enough of these 8 checks agree. Trend: Supertrend (10,3) is up, price is above VWAP, and EMA 9 is above EMA 21. Momentum: RSI(14) is between 50 and 75, the MACD histogram is positive, ADX(14) is at least 20 with +DI above −DI, and the 10-candle rate of change is positive with MACD still rising. Volume: the signal candle has at least 1.2× its 20-candle average volume, and today's volume is running at least level with a normal day at this time. A SELL (short) is the mirror image.
Volume column: RVOL is today's volume so far compared with the average of the last 10 sessions at the same time of day (2× means twice as busy as usual). Pressure is the share of the last 20 candles' volume that traded on candles moving in the signal's direction.
Momentum score (0–100) blends ADX trend strength, RSI, the +DI/−DI spread and relative volume. Extended means price is more than 1.5×ATR from EMA 9, so you'd be chasing. Wait for a pullback into the entry zone. Fading means RSI is overstretched or the MACD histogram has weakened for 2 candles.
Entry & exit plan: buy within the entry zone (signal close back toward EMA 9). The stop loss is the UT Bot trailing stop, widened to at least 1×ATR or 0.3% of price. At Target 1 (1.5× risk), book half and move the stop to your entry price. Let the rest run to Target 2 (3× risk). The entry, stop and targets always come from the signal candle, so a signal from 5 candles ago keeps its original plan. Trap filters: each signal shows whether the higher timeframe agrees (UT Bot and Supertrend on e.g. 15m for a 5m signal, or 1 week for daily), the breakout retest state (price broke the 20-candle high/low, came back to it and held: the classic professional entry), and trap warnings. The warnings cover a false breakout, an entry after a spike, a rejection wick, and stop-hunt risk (the stop sits just inside the previous day's low/high, a swing level or a round number, where stops cluster). An ⓘ note marks a resistance level before Target 1. Hover any badge for details, and use the three checkboxes above the table to filter. How long will it take? Under each target, "usually ~N candles" is how long that target typically took on this stock's own past UT Bot signals on the same timeframe, with how often it was hit. On intraday timeframes, targets are meant for the same session. On 1 day the estimate is in trading days, and on 1 week in weeks. Square off anything still open by 3:15 pm. Live Status follows each setup minute by minute on today's 1-minute candles: OPEN, T1 HIT, T2 HIT, SL HIT, or "exit at cost" after T1. If a minute touches both the stop and a target, the stop is assumed to have hit first.
Disclaimer: No indicator gives "exact" signals. These are rule-based setups, not investment advice. Prices come from Yahoo Finance and can be delayed by a few minutes. Always confirm on your broker's chart and size positions to your risk.

Key Value 2 with ATR 14 was in the top group on both timeframes, and the settings around it also did well. Compared with 1 / 10 it gave roughly 40–55% fewer signals, with a higher win rate and a better average result. The edge is modest and comes from one month of data, so it can change as market conditions change. Daily and weekly timeframes weren't tested.
R = the amount risked per trade (entry to stop loss). Brokerage, STT and slippage typically cost around 0.1–0.3R per intraday trade, so these results are roughly breakeven after costs.
